Research

Her research in developmental and educational psychology includes cross-cultural studies and developmental cognitive neuroscience.

Research areasResearch methods

Language development and parent–child interaction

Her work examines gestures, caregiver speech, and parent–child conversations in relation to children’s language and cognitive development. Cross-cultural studies focus on decontextualized language, including conversations about past and future events.

Related publications

  • Exploring the social origins of attention and executive functions: A cultural perspective from parent-infant interactions
  • From the “here and now” to the “there and then”: How parent–child decontextualized conversations support early development

Future thinking and self-regulation

Her research examines children’s future thinking and the regulation of behavior and emotion. Current projects investigate the cognitive and neural mechanisms of self-regulation and procrastination, and approaches to family-based intervention.

Related publications

  • Caregivers’ elaboration during joint narratives predicts Chinese children’s emotion regulation: Is theory of mind a mediator?
  • Kia Tīmata Pai (Best Start): A study protocol for a cluster randomised trial with early childhood teachers to support children’s oral language and self-regulation development

Artificial intelligence and child development

Her research examines the effects of artificial intelligence (AI) support on children’s narrative skills, including whether learning transfers to new stories. Current projects also investigate generative AI in family-based interventions.

Related publications

  • From scaffolding to transfer: The impact of AI-powered support on children’s narrative skills

Research methods

Methods include behavioral tasks, observations of parent–child interaction, eye tracking, electroencephalography (EEG), and analysis of children’s language environments using the Language Environment Analysis (LENA) system.
